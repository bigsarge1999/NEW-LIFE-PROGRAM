Ohm's Law

Voltage, current, and resistance are locked together for any single part:

V = I × R

Push more voltage across the same resistor and more current flows. Add more resistance and current drops.

Change any two fields — the third one solves itself.

Series circuits

One loop, one path. The same current flows through every part. Voltage splits up across each resistor in proportion to its resistance, and the resistances just add together.

R_total = R1 + R2 + …

Parallel circuits

Two or more branches share the same two nodes, so they all see the same voltage. Current splits between the branches — more flows through the lower-resistance path — and total resistance actually drops below the smallest branch.

1/R_total = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + …

The parts

Battery — pushes current around the loop at a fixed voltage. Every real battery has a little internal resistance, which is why this one warms up and sags slightly under heavy load, just like a truck battery under starter load.

Resistor — limits current. Bigger ohm value, less current for the same voltage. Undersized resistors cook — real ones carry a power rating in watts.

LED — only conducts one direction, and only once voltage across it clears its forward drop (about 2V here). Always needs a resistor in series or it draws too much current and burns out.

Switch — an open switch is an open road: no path, no current. Closed, it's just a wire.

Power

What actually turns into heat or light:

P = I × V
